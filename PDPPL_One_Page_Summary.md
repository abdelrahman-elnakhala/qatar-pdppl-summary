# Qatar PDPPL in One Page (Law No. 13 of 2016)

Plain-language summary for the HGMN portfolio projects. Draft dated 12 November 2026. Not legal advice.

**Verify before relying on this summary.** It was drafted from an unofficial English translation of the law (marked "not official", branded by the Compliance and Data Protection Department and hosted on database.cyberpolicyportal.org). The official text is the Arabic version in Official Gazette issue 15 of 2016, available on almeezan.qa, and it controls. Use `docs/verification_checklist.md` to check each row against it, and look for ministerial decisions and regulator guidelines issued since.

## Scope and key terms

- **Applies to** personal data that is processed electronically, or collected in preparation for electronic processing, or processed by a mix of electronic and traditional means (Art. 2).
- **Does not apply to** data processed by individuals for private or family purposes, or for official statistics under Law No. 2 of 2011 (Art. 2).
- **Controller** decides how and why personal data is processed. **Processor** processes it for the controller. **Personal data** relates to a person who is identified or reasonably identifiable (Art. 1).
- **Regulator:** the law gives enforcement to a "Competent Department" of the ministry then responsible for communications (Art. 1, Art. 26). Secondary sources now place this function within the National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA). Confirm the current name and contact point.

## What the law requires

| Topic | Plain-language reading | Article |
|---|---|---|
| Consent | Process personal data only with the individual's consent, unless processing is necessary for a lawful purpose of the controller or the recipient. | 4 |
| Individual rights | Withdraw consent, object, request erasure or correction, access their data, be told about processing and its purposes, and get a copy for a fee not above cost. Procedures are set by ministerial decision. | 5, 6, 7 |
| Transparency | Before processing, tell the individual who the controller is, the lawful purposes, and a description of the processing and disclosure. | 9 |
| Data quality | Keep data relevant, adequate, accurate and up to date, and no longer than necessary. | 10 |
| Controller duties | Review privacy measures before new processing, identify and train processors, run complaint and access-request systems, report breaches internally, audit compliance, and monitor processors. | 8, 11 |
| Security | Controllers and processors take precautions proportionate to the data. A processor must notify the controller of a breach immediately. | 13 |
| Breach notification | The controller informs the individual and the Competent Department when a breach may cause serious damage to personal data or privacy. | 14 |
| Cross-border flows | A controller may not restrict cross-border data flow, unless the processing breaches the law or may cause serious damage to privacy. | 15 |
| Special-nature data | Ethnic origin, children, health, physical or psychological condition, religious creeds, marital relations and criminal offences. Processing needs permission from the Competent Department. | 16 |
| Children's websites | Post a notice, get explicit consent from the guardian, and give the guardian access and deletion on request. | 17 |
| Exemptions | For authorities: national security, international relations, economic interests, crime prevention. For controllers: public-interest tasks, legal obligations or court orders, vital interests, public-interest scientific research, criminal investigations. | 18 to 21 |
| Direct marketing | Electronic marketing needs prior consent, must identify the sender, and must include a way to opt out. | 22 |
| Penalties | Fines up to QAR 1 million for breaches of Arts. 4, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15 and 22. Up to QAR 5 million for Art. 13, Art. 16 (para. 3) and Art. 17. Up to QAR 1 million for a legal person where offences are committed in its name or interest. | 23 to 25 |
| Complaints | Individuals may complain to the Competent Department, which can order a controller or processor to fix a breach. A grievance goes to the Minister within 60 days. | 26 |

## What it means for HGMN (fictional media network)

- **Subscriber data:** consent and notice at sign-up, retention limits and security controls map to Arts. 4, 9, 10 and 13.
- **Breach response:** a notification procedure is needed for the "serious damage" test in Art. 14. This links to risk R07 in the HGMN risk register.
- **Special-nature and source data:** health, children's or criminal-offence data falls under Art. 16. Confidential source data is not named in the law as a category, so classify it through internal policy.
- **Cloud, CDN and overseas bureaus:** Art. 15 limits restrictions on cross-border flows, and Art. 11 requires verifying processors. Contracts should reflect both.
- **Newsletters and children's content:** Art. 22 applies to marketing email and Art. 17 to any service directed at children.
- **Journalism:** the translation reviewed contains no specific exemption for journalistic activity. Check the Arabic text and any ministerial decisions before stating a position.
